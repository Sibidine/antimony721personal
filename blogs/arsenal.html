On Being an Arsenal Fan

Home Musings

This is a collection of passages from various points in the past when I had written about my mental state of being an Arsenal FC fan. The entries are presented in chronological order.


Dated 9/4/22:

It has all come crashing down, like a rude awakening from dreams of roses and orchards to the drudgery of unpaid rent and hostile bosses, taunting us with a sliver of hope — a glimpse of the light at the end of the tunnel — only for it to turn out to be the bulb of some land-dwelling angler fish ready to crunch through our dreams.

All the hope, the patience, the fervent prayers since the days of Mustafi and Lichtsteiner — of one day, while maybe not attaining our greatest highs, at least escaping our lowest of lows — and Fortune, the cruel mistress that she is, dangles the carrot, makes us dare to dream, dare to look up at the skies and rejoice that the worst is now past, only to stamp her heel down on our throats, relegating our thoughts to the doldrums of mid-table laughingstock again.

With this atavistic Lancelot devotion that we made too many moons ago to now forsake, our own primal fears and emotions are used to tantalize us, as if to make us bleed to death at a paper’s edge.

I don’t think I can take this anymore.


Dated 20/2/23:

While the exclamations of euphoria for last-minute winners against comparative minnows were once held as a barometer for our fall from grace, these very same wins now — having crossed the tumultuous sea of trauma and despair on our dingy raft held together by nothing but sheer mental fortitude and hope — resonate with this team’s newfound strength.

Hardship is known to metamorphose boys into men.

“Hard times make strong men, strong men make good times.”

The latter half of this proverb, whilst still being tucked away into the plausible future by pundits and peasants alike, might just already be here under our very noses. A team once considered too “soft,” “young,” “inexperienced” is now the only one seriously challenging the sportswashing Mancunian behemoth that has monopolised the “greatest league in the world.”

Perhaps the wheels employed by Mistress Fortune — for all her momentary crests and troughs — have already started turning, churning the dreary dough of despair that entrenched us long enough to establish it as our status quo for a generation of fans, into the warm, delectable, grandmotherly cookies of sweet, sweet joy.

Despite a run that can only be described as miraculous, our heads must not fly past the clouds, for the times call for the skill of Daedalus to forge our own fortune without the recklessness of Icarus flying too close to the sun. We mortal fans are not blessed with immaculate foresight. The only instruments we wield in this parade of dreams are hopes and aspirations that, after being locked in the cellars of mediocrity for eons, have finally been freed.

To quote Frost:

“And miles to go before I sleep, And miles to go before I sleep.”


Dated 14/4/24:

Pathos. It is the great filter, straining the chaff of fragility from the character of man, leaving naught but grains of resilience from which sprout the strength society is built on.

On the eve of a giant being felled in a faraway land by the sword of a fabled hero, we battle the very demons that have chronically beset our fortunes. Yet in this pathos of defeat — this fall from what seems to be grace — there is beauty to behold. The fog of adrenaline, once worn off, grants clarity of sight and mind.

These grains of resilience, once covered by the comfort of chaff, must hold firm now. A single fall in a joust decides not the battle’s outcome. Mayhap there is wisdom to be gained, complacency to be cut away from the comfort of success.

Even if we lose this long-drawn season, we must not lose sight of what lies beyond, nor ignore the road we’ve travelled — one so many have tried to replicate and failed. Note how many of similar stature would kill to be in our place.

And realise that sometimes — just sometimes — even as the peak remains elusive, the view from the mountain cliffs above the clouds, and the journey made so far, can yet be stunning.
